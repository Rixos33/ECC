# Style brief: what the reference channel does, and our twist

Source: two screen recordings of Kurzgesagt's Shorts (organs, water, never sleeping, dust, cocaine, itching)
plus their channel page, analysed 2026-10-02. We borrow the craft (pacing, structure, motion principles,
layout rules). Their characters, artwork, lettering and wording are theirs; ours must be original.

## Measured benchmarks (six reference Shorts vs our tickle Short)

| Measure | Reference range | Typical | Ours (round 9) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 61-77 s | ~70 s | 51 s |
| Words spoken | 182-260 | ~210 | 123 |
| Narration speed | 2.7-3.6 words/s | ~3.1 | 2.5 |
| Share of time with speech | 79-88% | ~85% | 71% |
| Pauses over 0.6 s | 2-10 | ~5 | 13 |
| Words per sentence (median) | 10-17 | ~13 | 9-10 |
| Large picture change | every 2.9-5.9 s | ~4 s | none (dissolves only) |
| Pixels changing per frame (median) | 2.6-6.7% | ~4% | 1.7% |
| Loudness | -14 LUFS | | -14 LUFS |

Reading: they talk faster and almost continuously, in longer sentences, and the picture moves about
twice as much. Comprehension comes from the picture carrying each idea, not from slow speech.

## How their Shorts are built

- Frame 0 is a title card: the question lettered on the artwork, the same image as the thumbnail.
- One subject fills 50-80% of the frame on a full-bleed colour world. No shared landscape template.
- The colour world changes with each beat: neon on deep navy for "inside the body" or space, bright
  pastels for everyday scenes, hot pink or orange for danger. Glows and soft gradients, no flat grey.
- Everything is a character: organs, cells and objects have faces and react to the narration.
- Things happen to the subject (it is cut, filled, zapped, grows) so each sentence is a visible event.
- Scenes flow into each other: zoom into an object and arrive in the next scene, wipes made by a moving
  shape, or one shape changing into another. Hard cuts are rare.
- Numbers live in the picture: fill levels, counters, a stat pill at the top of the frame.
- Labels are small tags with a pointer line. Jokes come as tiny speech bubbles.
- Captions: lowercase, rounded font, small (about 4% of frame height), white with a dark outline,
  1-3 words, centred at about 70% of the height.
- Structure is a ladder or a game: easy to hard, small to huge, with a twist near the end.
- Thumbnails: each is its own colour world with one character and 3-6 lettered words; side by side
  the grid looks like doors into different places, tied together by one drawing style.

Later recordings (black hole coin, sandwich, antimatter, cryonics, cube Earth, tiny black hole, lasers) fit the
same numbers: 50-62 s, 174-197 words, 3.0-3.5 words/s, a big picture change every 3-4 s. Two more devices:
numbered step tags ("1", "2", "3") for processes, and a two-state toggle for comparisons. They also use a
recurring host character, which supports our mascot.

## Our twist (owner's direction)

- Narration in the owner's own voice, with changes of tone (curious, dry, amazed), not one flat read.
- Mr. Shorts, the owl, as the recurring character: properly animated, one thinking activity per video,
  and the question that leads to the next Short.
- A topic title that stays at the top of the frame for the whole Short.
- Captions at the bottom, in step with the voice.
- The animation in the middle moves from one scene to the next without a break, in bright colour.
- Our own lettering, palette family and characters, so the channel looks like its own world.

## Topics about drugs (LSD, ADHD medication, MDMA, DMT, fentanyl)

Workable as science education (what it does in the brain and body, and why it is risky). Rules for scripts:
no dosing, sourcing, preparation or "how to take it"; no glamorising; state the risks plainly; sources in
the script file. Expect YouTube to age-restrict or limit ads on some of these even when done well.
